A calendar is made of the parts your `createCalendar` factory returns, such as `Calendar.Toolbar`,
and the views you register. This one puts every structural part together. `TeamCalendar` is the
calendar from the [views overview](/docs/views#registering-views), with an agenda and a month grid:

<include meta='title="team-calendar-app.tsx"'>../../examples/solid/anatomy.tsx#anatomy</include>

- **`Root`** holds the calendar's state and renders no element of its own. Every part and hook
  works anywhere inside it.
- **`Frame`** is the calendar's `<section>`. `Root`'s `label` names it, for screen readers and for
  an [agent](/docs/agents/connect#name-the-calendar).
- **`Toolbar`, `Title`, `Viewport`** and the three triggers each render one element. A trigger
  needs text or an `aria-label`.
- **`ViewSwitcher`** is a plain `<select>` with one option per registered view. To draw your own
  control, see [Switching views](/docs/views#switching-views).
- **`ActiveView`** renders the active view's `Renderer` inside the viewport.

The parts bring no styles. Each renders its element with a `data-calendar-part` attribute, such as
`data-calendar-part="today-trigger"`, so the look is your CSS. They take the element's own props in
Solid's spelling: `class`, not `className`. The
[component reference](/docs/api/components#components) lists every part.

## What the parts render

```html
<section data-calendar-part="frame" aria-label="Team calendar">
  <header data-calendar-part="toolbar">
    <button data-calendar-part="today-trigger">Today</button>
    <button data-calendar-part="previous-trigger" aria-label="Previous period">‹</button>
    <button data-calendar-part="next-trigger" aria-label="Next period">›</button>
    <span data-calendar-part="title">Oct 5 – 18, 2026</span>
    <select data-calendar-part="view-switcher" aria-label="View">
      …
    </select>
  </header>
  <div data-calendar-part="viewport">
    <!-- what the active view's Renderer returns -->
  </div>
</section>
```

Two more parts sit beside these. `Calendar.Event` looks one event up by its ID and hands it to its
children, and `Calendar.Announcer` is the live region that speaks
[keyboard and pointer gestures](/docs/accessibility#announcements).

## Views

`ActiveView` draws the rest, and a view's markup is its own. [Custom views](/docs/views/custom-views)
writes one from a strategy and a `Renderer`.
