---

The calendar shows events, and knows nothing about series. `@moltenship/calendar-recurrence` sits
beside it: it expands each series into its occurrences for the dates on screen, and the calendar
shows those as ordinary events. Views, drag and drop, and the event engine work the same with or
without it, and apps that don't import it pay nothing for it.

This page explains the model. [Show and edit recurring events](/docs/guides/recurring-events) wires
it into a calendar, and the [API reference](/docs/api/recurrence) lists every export.

## Series

Apps store repeating events the way iCalendar, Google Calendar and Outlook do:

- **A master event**, placed at the series' first occurrence, with its recurrence: a **rule**, such
  as "every weekday" (RRULE), **exception dates**, occurrences that don't happen (EXDATE), and
  **extra dates**, occurrences the rule doesn't give (RDATE).
- **Overrides**: separate events that each replace one occurrence, such as a meeting moved to
  another time. An override names its series and the occurrence's original start
  (RECURRENCE-ID). A **cancelled** override removes the occurrence, as Google's API and ICS feeds
  record a deleted one.
- **One-off events**: everything else.

```text
Stand-up (master)    Mon 19 Oct, 09:00–09:15      every weekday
  exception date     Wed 21 Oct, 09:00            no stand-up that day
Stand-up (override)  replaces Thu 22 Oct, 09:00   at 10:00–10:15

Occurrences, 19–23 Oct: Mon 09:00, Tue 09:00, Thu 10:00, Fri 09:00
```

Your events keep their own shape. A recurrence adapter, beside your event adapter, reads the
recurrence of a master and what an override replaces, as an
[event adapter](/docs/concepts/events) reads titles and times. Series can be endless, so occurrences
exist only for the dates you expand, usually the dates the calendar shows.

## Each series keeps its own time

Every date of a series has the kind of its first occurrence: an instant for a timed series, a date
for an all-day one, and a wall time for a floating one. That includes exception dates, extra dates,
the rule's end, and the original starts overrides name. A date of another kind converts, as real
feeds need: a date-only exception on a timed series excludes every occurrence that day, and a
date-only end lasts through that day.

A timed series repeats on the wall clock of its own time zone, the `sourceTimeZone` of its first
occurrence. So a 09:00 meeting stays at 09:00 across daylight saving time, and its instant moves by
an hour instead. A stand-up at 09:00 in Berlin is at 03:00 in New York most weeks, and at 04:00 in
the weeks between the two cities' clock changes. A floating series repeats at the same wall time in
every zone, and an all-day series on the same dates.

Some times and dates don't exist on a series' calendar:

- A start in a daylight saving gap, such as 02:30 on the night clocks go forward, moves forward by
  the gap and still counts. A start the clock repeats takes its first instant.
- Dates no month has, such as 30 February or the fifth Friday of a month with four, are skipped and
  don't count.
- The first occurrence is always one and counts toward the rule's COUNT, even when the rule
  wouldn't give it. Occurrences an exception date removes still count.

## Occurrence IDs

An occurrence's ID is its series' ID and its original start: `standup::2026-10-22T07:00:00Z`.
Instants are written in UTC, and dates and wall times as they are. The ID stays the same across
views, time zones and reloads, so you can store it, select it, and link to it.

An override keeps the original start of the occurrence it replaces, so an occurrence moved on its
own keeps its ID, and with it its selection and focus. An override shows under the same ID before
its master loads. One-off events keep their own IDs.

Changing the following occurrences, or all of them, changes their original starts, and so their
IDs. The edit maps old IDs to new ones, so your selection can follow; see
[Keep the selection](/docs/guides/recurring-events#keep-the-selection).

## Editing a series

When someone moves, resizes or deletes an occurrence, the app asks which events the change applies
to, then saves what that means for the series:

| Scope                     | A move or resize                                                         | A delete                        |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| This event                | An override for that occurrence, or a change to its override             | An exception date on the master |
| This and following events | The series ends before it, and a new series starts where it was dropped  | The series ends before it       |
| All events                | The whole series moves or stretches by the same change, on its own clock | The master and its overrides go |

- **All events** moves the rule with the change, such as Monday to Tuesday in a weekly rule, and
  moves exception dates, extra dates and the original starts overrides name, so deleted occurrences
  stay deleted and moved ones don't appear twice. Overrides keep their own times.
- **This and following events** splits the series, as Google Calendar and Outlook on the web do:
  later exception dates, extra dates and overrides move to the new series.
- Only scopes that can apply are offered. The first occurrence has no "This and following events",
  since that's all of them, and a rule that can't follow the change, such as "the 28th of each
  month" dragged to a 29th, offers only "This event".

## While the change saves

The change stays pending, and visible, while the app asks for the scope and saves it. Cancelling
the question rolls it back.

Only the occurrence that was dragged shows the change while it's pending. The calendar keeps
pending changes per event and knows nothing about series, so the rest of a series moves once your
events change and the series is expanded again.

## Limits

- **RANGE=THISANDFUTURE** isn't supported. Google Calendar and Outlook split series instead. The ICS
  bridge reports it and treats the override as one that replaces only its own occurrence.
- **Drags use the calendar's clock.** In a time zone whose daylight saving time changes on other
  dates than the series' zone, dragging an occurrence by days with "All events" moves the series'
  time by an hour, unless the edit gets the calendar's `timeZone`, which
  [the guide](/docs/guides/recurring-events#ask-which-events-a-change-applies-to) passes.
- **A repeated hour.** With "All events", a start dropped on the second of two equal wall times, as
  the hour repeated when clocks go back, lands on the first, as every occurrence of the series does.
- **Cancelled first occurrences.** Edits read one occurrence and its series, not the other
  overrides. When a cancelled override hides the first occurrence, "This and following events" from
  the second still splits the series rather than changing all of it.
- **The ISO calendar.** Rules repeat in the ISO calendar, iCalendar's Gregorian one: a monthly series
  steps by Gregorian months even when its dates use another calendar.
