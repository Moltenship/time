---
title: Handle daylight saving time
description: Days of 23 and 25 hours, wall-clock times the clocks skip or repeat, and the rows of a day view, with @moltenship/calendar-temporal.
---

Twice a year, in a zone with daylight saving time, a date isn't 24 hours long. In Berlin the
clocks go from 02:00 to 03:00 on March 29, 2026, so that date lasts 23 hours and 02:15 never
happens. On October 25 they go from 03:00 back to 02:00, so the date lasts 25 hours and 02:15
happens twice. Code that adds 24 hours to midnight, or that turns "02:15" into an instant without
looking, is wrong on those two dates.

The functions on this page take the time zone as an argument and never read the host's.

## How long a date is

`zonedDayRange(date, timeZone)` returns the real start and end of a date in a zone, and its length:

<include meta='title="daylight-saving-time.ts"'>../../examples/temporal-dst.ts#day</include>

In `Europe/Berlin`, `hoursInDay` returns 23 for March 29, 2026, 25 for October 25 and 24 for
October 8.

## Which events are on a date

`interval` on the result is the range of instants to compare events with. Compare with it, and not
with midnight plus 24 hours:

<include meta='title="daylight-saving-time.ts"'>../../examples/temporal-dst.ts#query</include>

An event from 22:30 to 23:00 UTC on October 8 is on October 9 in Berlin, where it starts at 00:30,
and on October 8 in New York.

`kind` is `"skipped"` in the rare case where a zone skips a whole date, as Samoa did on December
30, 2011. Such a date has no instants, and `interval` is `null`.

For an event that crosses midnight, `splitInstantRangeByZonedDay(range, timeZone)` cuts its range
into one piece per date, and each piece says whether the event continues before or after that
date.

## The rows of a day view

A day view shows the wall clock: 24 rows labelled 00:00 to 23:00, on every date. `slotsInZonedDay`
returns those rows with the instants behind each:

<include meta='title="daylight-saving-time.ts"'>../../examples/temporal-dst.ts#rows</include>

On March 29 in Berlin the 02:00 row is a gap and has no instant. On October 25 it is a fold and
has two:

```text
March 29, 2026                         October 25, 2026
01:00 unique 2026-03-29T00:00:00Z      01:00 unique 2026-10-24T23:00:00Z
02:00 gap                              02:00 fold   2026-10-25T00:00:00Z 2026-10-25T01:00:00Z
03:00 unique 2026-03-29T01:00:00Z      03:00 unique 2026-10-25T02:00:00Z
```

A slot that a clock change cuts through is split at the change: its `kind` is `"transition"` and it
has one segment for each part. The function throws a `RangeError` when a day would have more than
10,000 slots, and `limit` raises that.

## A time a person typed

A form gives a date and a time with no zone. `resolveZonedWallTime` turns them into an instant and
says what it found: `kind` is `"unique"`, `"gap"` or `"fold"`.

<include meta='title="daylight-saving-time.ts"'>../../examples/temporal-dst.ts#pick</include>

With Berlin as the zone:

| The person typed  | `pickTime` returns                                  |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| October 8, 02:15  | The instant `2026-10-08T00:15:00Z`                  |
| March 29, 02:15   | "The clocks skip that time. Did you mean 03:15?"    |
| October 25, 02:15 | The instant `2026-10-25T00:15:00Z`, the first 02:15 |

`disambiguation` takes Temporal's four values. `"compatible"` moves a skipped time later by the
length of the gap and picks the earlier of two repeated times. `"reject"` gives a result whose
`status` is `"rejected"` for both. In a fold, `occurrences` holds both instants, so a form can ask
which one the person meant.

## A pointer on a time grid

A drag gives a wall-clock time that needs rounding to the grid. `snapZonedWallTime` rounds it and
resolves it in one step:

<include meta='title="daylight-saving-time.ts"'>../../examples/temporal-dst.ts#snap</include>

09:07 on October 8 snaps to 09:00. 02:10 on March 29 has no 02:00 or 02:15 to snap to, so it lands
on 03:00, the moment the clocks change. `mode` is `"nearest"` by default, or `"floor"` or `"ceil"`,
and `fold` picks the earlier or the later of two repeated times.
