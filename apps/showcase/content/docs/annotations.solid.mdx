---
title: Annotations
description: Time-based information beside events, such as listening history, sleep, workouts and focus sessions.
---

Annotations show what happened alongside the events: what you listened to, when you slept, a
workout, a focus session. Unlike events they are read-only, and they never take part in overlap
layout, so they never reorder an event or change its lane.

The calendar doesn't fetch anything. Your app loads the data from its integrations and passes it to
`Root`, the same way it passes events.

Pass both to `Root`. `Tracker` here is a calendar with the week view that
[Draw them in your own view](#draw-them-in-your-own-view) writes. With the registry's week, pass
them the same way and [the week and day](#the-week-and-day) draw them:

<include meta='title="annotations.tsx"'>../examples/solid/annotations.tsx#root</include>

The library knows no kinds of source: "sleep" and "music" are your names. Sources come in the order
you list them, and leaving one out of `annotationSources` hides its annotations. Like events,
annotations are half-open, `[start, end)`. Invalid ones are skipped and reported through
`onDiagnosticsChange`.

## The week and day

The registry's week and day views draw annotations as soon as `Root` has sources. Without any,
they draw no rail. Give each source a `color` from the event palette and an `Icon`:

<include meta='title="week-annotations.tsx"'>
  ../examples/solid/week-annotations.tsx#sources
</include>

- **A rail beside the events.** The marks draw in a narrow strip at the start of each day column,
  18px in the week and 30px in the day. An event moves aside for the rail only where a mark is
  beside it, counting a short mark as tall as the rail's `markWidth`. The events it shares lanes
  with move with it, so they keep their places beside each other. Every other event, and every
  event on a day without marks, keeps the column's whole width. With `placement: "overlay"` the
  parts draw behind events instead and move nothing.
- **Capsules with the source's icon.** Each annotation draws as a capsule in its source's color,
  with the icon inside its top. A mark is never shorter than it is wide, so a short session is a
  circle with the icon.
- **Merging.** A source's annotations closer than its `mergeGap` draw as one mark, such as tracks
  into a listening session. Marks whose circles would touch merge too, so song-level history stays
  readable when zoomed out.
- **Lanes.** When different sources overlap, such as music during a focus session, they share the
  rail in up to three narrower lanes. Too narrow for an icon, a mark drops it.
- **Days.** An annotation across midnight shows on both days. A part that starts the day before
  fades in at the top and has no icon.
- **Details.** Hovering a mark shows a tooltip, and clicking it opens a popover listing what it
  merges. Both are the kit's, in `components/calendar/ui/`, one of each for the whole grid. The
  popover keeps focus until Escape, an outside click or its close button, then returns it to where
  it was. Pressing or dragging a mark never starts a new event.

### Customize them

The rail is registry source like the rest of the view, built from small parts you can restyle,
recompose or edit. From the lightest change to the heaviest:

**CSS.** Each mark has `data-slot="annotation-mark"`, `data-source`, `data-color`, `data-kind`
(`bar` or `dot`), and `data-starts-before` or `data-ends-after` when it's cut at midnight. This
gives sleep a night sky:

```css
[data-slot="annotation-mark"][data-source="sleep"] {
  background: linear-gradient(to bottom, oklch(0.5 0.19 282), oklch(0.7 0.14 275));
}
```

**Your own composition.** Pass a component as the view's `annotations.Annotations`. It gets one
column's marks, already merged and placed in lanes, and composes the parts:

<include meta='title="annotation-parts.tsx"'>
  ../examples/solid/annotation-parts.tsx#composition
</include>

- `AnnotationMark` has the variants `capsule` (default), `bar` (a thin bar that keeps its true
  length) and `band` (a tint across the column behind events, with `placement: "overlay"`). Its
  `render` prop takes a function of the mark's props that returns your element in its place, such
  as a link to the track. Spread the props on it: they place it and wire its tooltip and popover.
- `AnnotationMarkIcon` draws the source's `Icon`. Children replace it.
- `useAnnotationMark()` gives any part inside a mark its mark and width.

**Rail size and lanes.** These are numbers rather than CSS, because merging and lane packing use
them:

<include meta='title="annotation-parts.tsx"'>../examples/solid/annotation-parts.tsx#rail</include>

**The parts themselves.** `time-grid-annotations.tsx` is yours: change the capsule, the tooltip or
the popover there.

### Keyboard and screen readers

Annotations add no keys and no tab stops: the grid keeps its single tab stop and its
[key map](/docs/views/week-and-day#keyboard). The rail is `aria-hidden`, and screen readers get the
same information in words instead:

| Where            | What a screen reader hears                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A day's header   | A summary of its annotations: "Annotations: Sleep until 7:05 AM. Music, 3 times, 2 hours, 10 minutes."         |
| A slot, with ↑ ↓ | The items drawn across it, after its time: "Music: Nightcall, 9:00 AM to 9:04 AM; Genesis, 9:05 AM to 9:09 AM" |

Hover effects respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Draw them in your own view

`useTimeGridAnnotations(columns, geometry)` lays the annotations out beside a time grid's columns
and returns an accessor of one list of marks per column. This week view draws them in a rail at the
start of each day:

<include meta='title="annotations.tsx"'>../examples/solid/annotations.tsx#view</include>

Register the view, and the `Root` above renders it:

<include meta='title="annotations.tsx"'>../examples/solid/annotations.tsx#calendar</include>

A mark is one source's annotations merged and placed, ready to draw:

- **Position.** `top` and `height` are fractions of the column's 24-hour axis, like an event's
  layout. `kind` is `"dot"` for a mark shorter than the geometry's `minHeight`, which draws at that
  height, and `"bar"` for the rest.
- **Merging.** A source's annotations closer than its `mergeGap` are one mark, such as tracks in a
  listening session. Marks closer than the geometry's `minGap` merge too, so song-level history
  stays readable when zoomed out. `mark.annotations` lists what a mark merges, in time order.
- **Lanes.** When different sources overlap, such as music during a focus session, they share the
  rail: `lane` is the mark's lane and `lanes` how many it shares the rail with.
- **Days.** An annotation across midnight gives a mark on both days. `startsBeforeDay` and
  `endsAfterDay` say where a mark is cut, so you can fade that end.

Style the marks with your own CSS. The sample puts the source's ID in `data-source`:

```css
.rail {
  position: absolute;
  inset-block: 0;
  inset-inline-start: 0;
  inline-size: 18px;
}

.mark {
  position: absolute;
  border-radius: 999px;
  background: oklch(0.7 0.15 150);
}

.mark[data-source="sleep"] {
  background: linear-gradient(to bottom, oklch(0.5 0.19 282), oklch(0.7 0.14 275));
}
```

The sample's rail is `aria-hidden`, so say the same in words. `summarizeAnnotationMarks` returns a
day's totals per source, in source order, for a day's header or a screen-reader summary:

<include meta='title="annotations.tsx"'>../examples/solid/annotations.tsx#summary</include>

`useCalendarAnnotations()` returns the root's annotations and sources as they are, for a view that
isn't a time grid.

## Geometry

The second argument of `useTimeGridAnnotations`. Every value is optional.

A source has an `id`, a `label` that names it in summaries and tooltips, and an optional
`mergeGap`. A mark carries the source object you passed, so a source can hold more, such as a
color.
