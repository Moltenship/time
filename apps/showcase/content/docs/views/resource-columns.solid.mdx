The [Solid demo](https://moltenship-time-demo.vercel.app/solid/?view=resources) opens on the
resource columns with three rooms and two teams, each with whether it's free now.

`createResourceColumnsView` shows one time-grid column for each resource, such as a room or a
person, for the anchor date. Pass a number of days to give each resource that many day columns.
Resources with children appear as groups that collapse. Only the columns in view are rendered, so
the view stays fast with hundreds of resources.

## Add it

Add the `resource-columns-view` item to your app with the shadcn CLI, as
[UI source](/docs/installation#ui-source-optional) describes. It brings `time-grid-view`, whose
columns, events and all-day row it reuses, the theme, the shared `calendar` parts, and
`@tanstack/virtual-core`:

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add @moltenship/resource-columns-view
```

Then register the view. This one draws a person's initial before their name and stacks a room's
bookings:

The view is named `resources`. Previous and Next move by its number of days:
`createResourceColumnsView<Event>(options, 3)` shows three days for each resource. The columns
scroll both ways inside the height they're given, so give `Calendar.Viewport` a height:

## Resources

Pass the resources to `Root`. Each one has an `id`, a `title`, and optionally a `parentId`, which
puts it in a group under that resource. Events list the resources they book in `resourceIds`, read
through your event adapter's `getResourceIds`. An event that books several resources shows in each
of their columns.

<include meta='title="resources.ts"'>
  ../../examples/solid/resource-columns-page.tsx#resources
</include>

A group's children show while its ID is in `expandedResourceIds`. The chevron on a group's header
toggles it. To control expansion yourself, pass `expandedResourceIds` and
`onExpandedResourceIdsChange`. Groups start collapsed unless you list them in
`defaultExpandedResourceIds`.

A change that moves an event to another resource proposes its new `resourceIds`, so saving
`change.proposed`, as the page above does, keeps the event in the column it was dropped on.

## Avatars and icons

Columns often mix people and rooms, and a bare name doesn't say which is which. To show it, pass
your own `ResourceHeader`, as the calendar above does. It draws each header's name row from small
parts, so you can put a person's avatar or a room's icon before the name. Keep what the header
needs in each resource's `data`, and give it a type: the view takes it as its second type
parameter, so the component reads it from `props.resource`.

The parts live in `resource-header.tsx`:

- `ResourceHeaderToggle` is a group's chevron. It renders nothing for a resource without children.
- `ResourceHeaderIcon` wraps anything you draw before the name. Screen readers skip it, because the
  name beside it already says who or what the column is.
- `ResourceHeaderTitle` is the name, cut short with an ellipsis. Children replace it.
- `ResourceHeaderTime` is the resource's local time, while its `timeZone` keeps another clock than
  the calendar's.
- `ResourceHeaderStatus` is its status, with the view's `resourceStatus`, such as "Busy until 3:00
  PM".
- `useResourceHeader()` gives your own parts the resource, its name, its groups and its status.
  `DefaultResourceHeader` is the toggle, the title, and the time or the status under it.

`resource` is `null` for the Unassigned column. The view keeps the rest of the header: the group
bands, the row heights and the group names screen readers hear. A row is 2rem tall, so keep
icons at 24px (`size-6`) or smaller.

## Rooms and people

Rooms and people often want different layouts. A room's bookings can stack, so a busy room reads
at a glance, while a person's meetings sit side by side. `getResourceOverlapLayout` gives each
resource's columns a layout of its own; it takes the resource with your `data` type, so it can read
what kind it is, as the calendar above does.

The most specific layout wins, and each `undefined` falls through to the next:

1. An event's own layout, from `getEventOverlapLayout`, such as its calendar's. A group of
   overlapping events takes it only when they all agree, as in the
   [week and day views](/docs/views/week-and-day).
2. The column's resource, from `getResourceOverlapLayout`.
3. The view's `overlapLayout`, then `"columns"`.

A group whose events disagree takes its column's layout, from 2 or 3. The Unassigned column isn't
a resource, so it follows `overlapLayout`. An event that books a room and a person is laid out in
each column by that column's layout, and a move to another resource redraws it there by the new
one's.

Whether a room may be double-booked at all is a separate rule, with a mode per resource too: see
[double booking](/docs/availability#double-booking).

## What it shows

- **Header:** each resource's name, or what your `ResourceHeader` draws. An expanded group's band
  names it across its own column and its children's columns. Over several days, a row under the
  names shows each day's weekday and date.
- **All-day row:** each resource's all-day events, and timed events of 24 hours or more. Busy days
  collapse to three rows with "+N more" in every column at once, so the row keeps one height.
- **Columns:** the same hours, events, daylight-saving bands and now line as the
  [day view](/docs/views/week-and-day). With [availability](/docs/availability), each column shades
  its own resource's closed time.
- **Scrolling:** columns share the width until they are 128px per day, then the view scrolls
  sideways. The header, the all-day row and the hour gutter follow the body's scroll.
- **Editing:** with `onEventChange` on `Root`, drag an event within its column to move or resize it,
  or into another resource's column to move it there. A move swaps the resource you dragged from
  for the one you dropped on and keeps the event's other resources. Drag across empty time or
  all-day cells to create an event in that resource.
- **Accessible names:** events, slots and all-day cells include their resource, as in "Design
  review, Thursday, October 8, 10:00 – 11:30 AM, Orchid". Announcements name it too.
- **On a phone** every event and button is at least 24 px wide, a long press doesn't select text,
  and the columns keep their 128px, so the view scrolls sideways.
- **Right to left** mirrors the columns, the header and the gutter when the page or
  `Calendar.Frame` has `dir="rtl"`.
- **Reduced motion** moves the drag preview without its glide.

## Anatomy

`ResourceColumnsView` renders the parts below. The view builds them itself from its projection, so
they're not parts you compose: to change one, edit its file under
`components/calendar/resource-columns-view`. The exception is each header's name row, which you
can replace with [`ResourceHeader`](#avatars-and-icons).

```txt title="Rendered by ResourceColumnsView"
ResourceColumnsView              resource-columns-view.tsx
├── ResourceColumnsHeader        resource-columns-header.tsx
│   └── Resource header          one per rendered resource
│       └── ResourceHeader       resource-header.tsx, or your own composition
├── ResourceColumnsDates         resource-columns-dates.tsx, over N days
├── ResourceColumnsAllDay        resource-columns-all-day.tsx
│   └── TimeGridAllDayCells      one set per rendered resource
└── ResourceColumnsBody          resource-columns-body.tsx, scrolls both ways
    ├── Time gutter              --calendar-gutter-width, with secondary time zones
    └── Resource column          data-resource-id
        └── TimeGridColumn       time-grid-column.tsx, one per day
            ├── TimeGridEvent    time-grid-event.tsx
            └── Now line         data-slot="time-grid-now"
```

The `.tsx` files are Solid components. What they do comes from plain TypeScript beside them, the
same files React's resource columns read:

| File                         | Holds                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `column-virtualizer.ts`      | Which columns render, which `use-column-virtualizer.ts` binds         |
| `axis-sync.ts`               | How the header and gutter follow the body, bound by `use-axis-sync`   |
| `focused-column.ts`          | Scrolling a focused resource into view, bound by `use-focused-column` |
| `resource-columns-labels.ts` | Words such as "Unassigned"                                            |
| `resource-columns-layout.ts` | Sizes and the snap                                                    |
| `resource-columns.styles.ts` | Every class the parts set, so a restyle is one file                   |

## Keyboard

Tab into the example above, or click a slot, and try these keys. Columns run resource by resource:
each resource's days, then the next resource. Inside a column the keys are the
[day view's](/docs/views/week-and-day#keyboard). The
[Accessibility](/docs/accessibility#keyboard) page has the full map.

| Focus         | Key                | Effect                                                                                       |
| ------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A day or slot | ← →                | Same slot in the previous or next column, crossing into the next resource                    |
| A day or slot | ← → past the ends  | On to the day before or after the range, in the last or first resource                       |
| A day or slot | Home, End          | First or last column                                                                         |
| A day or slot | Page Up, Page Down | Same resource, a whole day (or range) earlier or later                                       |
| A day or slot | ↑ ↓, Enter         | As in the day view: slots, the all-day cell, and the events covering a slot                  |
| A day or slot | Space              | Start an event there, in that column's resource                                              |
| An event      | Space              | Pick it up. ← → move it across columns, and into another resource it swaps that one resource |

Focus can move to a resource that's scrolled out of view: its column renders and scrolls in, and
it stays rendered while you scroll away, so focus is never lost.

## Build your own

The strategy is exported from `@moltenship/calendar-solid`: `resourceColumnsStrategy`, with
`resourceColumnsSpatialNavigation` for the keys. Its projection has the visible resources, each
with its days, its all-day rows and its timed columns. See [Custom views](/docs/views/custom-views).
Each resource's cells sit inside an element with `resourceIdAttributes(id)`, which sets
`data-resource-id`: a drop or create inside it lands in that resource.
