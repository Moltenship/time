The [Solid demo](https://moltenship-time-demo.vercel.app/solid/?view=week) opens on the week, with
more events and a toolbar.

`createWeekView` and `createDayView` show the hours of the day down the side and one column per day.
The week view has seven columns, starting on `weekRules.firstDay`. The day view has one column for
the anchor date. `createThreeDayView` has three, from the anchor date: on a phone, where a week's
seven columns are too narrow to read, it shows more than a day.

<Callout type="warn" title="Annotations and working hours come next">
  The week and day don't draw the root's annotations or shade time outside working hours yet.
</Callout>

## Add them

Add the `time-grid-view` item to your app with the shadcn CLI, as
[UI source](/docs/installation#ui-source-optional) describes. It brings the theme, the shared
`calendar` parts and the kit's tooltip:

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add @moltenship/time-grid-view
```

Then register the views:

The views are named `week`, `day` and `3-day`. Previous and Next move by a week, a day or three
days. The hours scroll inside the height they're given, so give `Calendar.Viewport` a height, and
save the changes a drag or a key proposes in `onEventChange`:

## What it shows

- **Header:** the weekday and date of each column, with today highlighted and marked
  `aria-current="date"`. The corner shows the time zone's offset, such as "GMT+2", after the name
  of each [secondary time zone](#secondary-time-zones).
- **All-day row:** all-day events, plus timed and floating events that last 24 hours or more. Busy
  days collapse to three rows with "+N more", and a chevron in the corner expands the row.
- **Hours:** labels come from `Intl`, so they read "1 PM" or "13:00" depending on the locale. Each
  hour is `--calendar-hour-height` tall (3rem by default).
- **Events:** overlapping events sit side by side, or as `overlapLayout` sets. Events shorter than
  45 minutes show on one line, as "Standup, 9:30 AM". Events that run past midnight show their real
  times on both days, as "10:00 PM – 2:00 AM". A title that doesn't fit its event shows in full in
  the kit's tooltip while you hover or focus the event.
- **Daylight saving time:** hours skipped when the clocks go forward are hatched, and hours repeated
  when they go back are tinted, with both occurrences side by side.
- **Current time:** a line across today's column, from
  [`useNow()`](/docs/concepts/time#the-current-time). The grid opens scrolled to the current time,
  or to 08:00 when today isn't visible, and keeps its scroll position when you move between weeks.
- **Editing:** with `onEventChange` on `Root`, drag an event to move it (onto the all-day row to
  make it all-day), drag its top or bottom edge to resize it, and drag across empty time or all-day
  cells to create one. See [Interactions](/docs/interactions#drag-resize-and-create).
- **Changes being saved:** events with a pending change, the ones
  [`useCalendarPendingChanges`](/docs/api/components#hooks) returns, get `aria-busy` and render at
  60% opacity.
- **On a phone** every event and button is at least 24 px wide, and a long press doesn't select
  text. The 3-day view fits a phone better than the week.
- **Right to left** mirrors the grid, the hours and the events' borders included, when the page or
  `Calendar.Frame` has `dir="rtl"`.
- **Reduced motion** opens the tooltip without its scale and slide.

## Overlapping events

`overlapLayout` sets how events that overlap share a day column:

- **`"columns"`** (the default) puts overlapping events side by side, in as few lanes as they
  need. Each event widens into lanes that stay free beside it.
- **`"equal"`** uses the same lanes, but every event in a busy stretch is one lane wide, so the
  lanes line up.
- **`"stack"`** cascades them. Each event is indented past the earlier events it overlaps and drawn
  on top of them, reaching to the column's end. `stackIndent` sets the indent. A deep stack indents
  less, so the top event keeps at least half the column.

`getEventOverlapLayout` gives events a layout of their own, usually their calendar's, and returns
`undefined` for events that follow the view's. A group of overlapping events takes its events' own
layout only when they all agree, and otherwise the view's `overlapLayout`.

Every layout keeps the same rules:

- Events that touch, one ending as the next starts, don't overlap. An event shorter than 15
  minutes still takes up 15 so it stays readable, so it can overlap the next one.
- Overlapping events form a group, laid out on its own, so a change to one group never moves
  events in another.
- The order is fixed: by start, then longer first, then event ID. It's the order events are drawn
  in.

## Secondary time zones

Pass `secondaryTimeZones` to `Root` to label the hours in other time zones too, such as
`secondaryTimeZones={["America/New_York", { timeZone: "Asia/Tokyo", label: "Tokyo" }]}`. Each zone
gets a column of hours beside the calendar's own.

- **Names:** each column is named by its `label`, or by the zone's short name from `Intl` in the
  calendar's locale, such as "EDT" or "GMT+5:30". Without a `label`, hover it for the zone's full
  name.
- **Hours:** each line is read on its own date, in both zones' rules, so the labels stay right when
  either zone changes its clocks. Zones off the hour show minutes, such as "4:30 PM" in India.
  "+1" or "-1" marks a time on the next or previous day there.
- **Daylight saving time:** an hour the calendar's zone skips has no label, and an hour it repeats
  shows its first time. A day that reads differently, because a zone changes its offset, says so
  under its date, such as "EST -1h". Hover the note to see from when, and until when if it ends
  that day.
- **Width:** each zone's column is `--calendar-zone-width` wide, 4rem by default, which fits
  "12:30 AM+1". Set the variable on an element around the calendar for a locale that writes longer
  times, such as `[--calendar-zone-width:4.5rem]`.
- **Screen readers:** the extra hours are hidden, like the calendar's own hour labels, so the grid's
  columns stay the same.

[Time and time zones](/docs/concepts/time#secondary-time-zones) explains how each line is read.

## Anatomy

All three views render `TimeGridView`, with seven day columns for the week, three for the 3-day
view and one for the day. The view builds these parts itself from its projection, so they're not
parts you compose: to change one, edit its file under `components/calendar/time-grid-view`.

```txt title="Rendered by TimeGridView"
TimeGridView                     time-grid-view.tsx, a grid
├── TimeGridHeader               time-grid-header.tsx, the time zone and each day
├── TimeGridAllDay               time-grid-all-day.tsx, the all-day row
│   ├── AllDayRowHeader          "All day", and the chevron that expands the row
│   └── AllDayCell               a gridcell per day; its drop layer has data-date and data-all-day
│       ├── AllDayEvent          one per event in the row
│       └── "+N more"            expands the row
└── TimeGridBody                 time-grid-body.tsx, scrolls
    ├── Time gutter              --calendar-gutter-width
    │   └── TimeGridZoneHours    time-grid-zones.tsx, one per secondary time zone
    └── TimeGridColumn           time-grid-column.tsx, one per day, data-date
        ├── TimeGridEvent        time-grid-event.tsx
        │   ├── Title and time
        │   └── Resize handles   data-resize="start" and "end"
        ├── Draft                data-slot="time-grid-draft", a keyboard create's range
        ├── Slot                 the focused 15-minute slot, while one has focus
        └── Now line             data-slot="time-grid-now"
```

The `.tsx` files are Solid components. What they draw comes from plain TypeScript beside them:

| File                    | Holds                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `time-grid.styles.ts`   | Every class the parts set, so a restyle is one file                                   |
| `time-grid-labels.ts`   | The dates, hours, ranges and names the parts show and read out                        |
| `time-grid-geometry.ts` | Where an event, a gap or a fold sits in its column, and where the grid opens scrolled |
| `all-day-layout.ts`     | Which all-day events fit in a collapsed row, and how many each day hides              |
| `secondary-zones.ts`    | Each zone's hours, names and the notes under a day whose times differ                 |

- The gutter is `--calendar-gutter-width` wide: `--calendar-hours-width` for the hours (3rem, or
  4rem from the `sm` breakpoint), plus `--calendar-zone-width` for each secondary time zone.
- An event's `top` and `height` come from its times, and its `left` and `width` from the
  [overlap layout](#overlapping-events). A column renders its events in layout order, so a stacked
  event draws over the ones it overlaps.
- The grid shares one tooltip among its events, so a week of events renders one tooltip, not one
  each.

## Keyboard

The grid is one tab stop. The [Accessibility](/docs/accessibility#keyboard) page has the full map
and what a screen reader hears.

| Focus           | Key                | Effect                                                                            |
| --------------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| An all-day cell | ↓                  | First slot of the day                                                             |
| A slot          | ↑ ↓                | Previous or next 15-minute slot. Up from the first slot reaches the all-day cell  |
| A day or slot   | ← →                | Same slot on the previous or next day                                             |
| A day or slot   | Home, End          | First or last day shown                                                           |
| A day or slot   | Page Up, Page Down | Back or forward a whole week (or day)                                             |
| A slot          | Enter              | The event covering it. Escape goes back to the slot it starts in                  |
| An all-day cell | Enter              | Go into the day's all-day events                                                  |
| A timed event   | ↑ ↓, ← →           | Previous or next event in the column, or the event beside it that overlaps it     |
| A day or slot   | Shift+Enter        | Open that date in the day view, when the week has `dayView`                       |
| A slot          | Space              | Start an hour-long event there. Arrow keys extend it, and Enter saves it          |
| An all-day cell | Space              | Start an all-day event there                                                      |
| An event        | Space              | Pick it up. Arrow keys move it by slots and days, Enter saves, and Escape cancels |
| An event        | Shift+Space        | Pick up its end to resize it. Shift+Space again switches to its start             |

Moving a picked-up timed event above the first slot makes it all-day, and moving an all-day event
below the all-day row makes it timed. Only the focused slot is an element, so a week's 672 slots
cost nothing until you visit them. After a drop, focus stays on the moved event.

## Markup for interactions

The view connects the pointer layer for you. If you change its markup, keep the attributes the
pointer layer reads: day columns have `data-date` and cover the full 24 hours, each all-day cell's
drop layer has `data-date` and `data-all-day`, each event is a `<button>` with `data-event-id`, and
events you can edit have `data-resize="start"` and `data-resize="end"` handles.
