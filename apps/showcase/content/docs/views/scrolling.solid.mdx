The [week and day](/docs/views/week-and-day), [month](/docs/views/month) and
[agenda](/docs/views/agenda) views move a page at a time. Their scrolling versions follow your
finger, as Apple's Calendar does on iPad. The week and day views swipe sideways, so a week comes to
rest on the next week and a day on the next day. The month swipes up and down a month at a time,
and the agenda scrolls one list of days through months or years, comes to rest on any day, and
jumps to a date at once. They're separate views, so the paged ones keep working as they are, and
you can register both.

The [Solid demo](https://moltenship-time-demo.vercel.app/solid/?view=scrolling-week) has them all:
turn on "Scroll days, weeks, months and agenda" in its settings.

## Add them

Add the items to your app with the shadcn CLI, as
[UI source](/docs/installation#ui-source-optional) describes. Each brings the view it builds on,
`time-grid-view`, `month-view` or `list-view`, with the theme and the shared `calendar` parts:

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add @moltenship/scrolling-time-grid-view @moltenship/scrolling-month-view @moltenship/scrolling-list-view
```

Then register the views:

The views are named `scrolling-week`, `scrolling-3-day`, `scrolling-day`, `scrolling-month` and
`scrolling-agenda`, and take the same options as their paged versions. `dayView` names the view a
date opens; it defaults to `"day"` in the month and agenda. For another number of days, define a
view with `scrollingTimeGridStrategy({ count, kind: "days" })` and `ScrollingTimeGridView`, as
`createScrollingThreeDayView` does; it swipes that many days at a time.

## How they move

| View               | Shows                                                                                               | A swipe moves | Previous and Next | Today                     |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| `scrolling-week`   | The anchor's week, from `weekRules`                                                                 | A week        | A week            | Today's week              |
| `scrolling-3-day`  | Three days from the anchor date                                                                     | Three days    | Three days        | Today in the first column |
| `scrolling-day`    | The anchor date                                                                                     | A day         | A day             | Today                     |
| `scrolling-month`  | Six weeks from the week of the 1st, or up to twelve with `createScrollingMonthView(options, weeks)` | A month       | A month           | Today's month             |
| `scrolling-agenda` | `weeks` from the anchor date, four by default                                                       | Free, any day | `weeks`           | Today on top              |

The anchor date is the scroll position. When a scroll comes to rest, the view sets `anchorDate` to
what it shows, so `Calendar.Title`, `onAnchorDateChange`, and a URL that stores the date follow the
scroll. During a long swipe the anchor follows every half page, so the next weeks load before they
scroll into view. With a controlled `anchorDate`, the view stays where it was scrolled until your
state takes the latest date `onAnchorDateChange` sent, even a few flushes later; any other
`anchorDate` moves the view to it. If you defer the update, commit the latest date rather than each
one in turn.

### The week and day

A swipe sideways follows your finger and comes to rest on the next page, or back on this one if it
went less than halfway; a fling moves one page. A swipe up or down scrolls the hours instead: the
days and the hours scroll in two elements, so a swipe moves one of them, whichever way it starts.
The header, the all-day row and the hour gutter stay in place while the days scroll under them,
and all-day bars keep their titles on screen while their start is scrolled past. The hours open on
the current time, or on 08:00 when no rendered day shows it.

When the anchor changes any other way, from Today, Next, or your own `setAnchorDate`, the days
scroll there: smoothly over a page or two, and at once from further away, from the keyboard, or
when the system asks for reduced motion.

first day, which every month fits, with the days of other months dimmed. A swipe follows your finger

### The agenda

<SolidExample
  name="scrolling-agenda"
  title="The scrolling agenda"
  size="h-[34rem]"
  caption="Live. It loads events from a pretend server as you scroll, and the end of the list says so while they're on their way."
/>

- **The anchor date is the day on top.** The range starts at the anchor date rather than its week,
  since the list comes to rest on any day.
- **Today, Previous, Next, and any other anchor date jump the list** to put that date on top, or
  the next day with events, at once, rather than gliding there as the month does. The list holds
  that date on top while its events load, until you scroll.
- **Only the days in view render.** Each is measured once it renders, as its events wrap, and days
  that load above or below the ones on screen don't move them.
- **The end of the list** follows the last loaded day. It's as tall as the view, so any day can
  scroll to the top, and says "Loading events…" while `loading` is set, or that nothing more is
  scheduled. Scrolling on past either end of the loaded days moves a page that way, once per
  gesture, so the list crosses weeks without events. Weeks shown without events get the empty
  state, at the end of the list or among the days after them.

## Loading events as you scroll

Each scrolling view renders a page of weeks on either side of what it shows, with their events, so
a swipe never reveals an empty page. Its strategy reports those dates as its **load range**, and
`onVisibleRangeChange` on `Root` hands them to you: after the first render, and whenever the view,
its dates, or the time zone change, but not when only `events` do.

Fetch events for `loadRange` and pass them in `events`. Set the agenda's `loading` while some are on
their way:

<include meta='title="scrolling-agenda-page.tsx"'>
  ../../examples/solid/scrolling-agenda-page.tsx#loading
</include>

| Field              | Contents                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `view`             | The active view's name                                       |
| `range`            | The visible dates                                            |
| `instantRange`     | `range` as instants in the calendar's time zone              |
| `loadRange`        | The dates to have events for: `range` and a page either side |
| `loadInstantRange` | `loadRange` as instants, ready to send to an API             |

Paged views report too: their `loadRange` is the visible `range`. Time never fetches or keeps
events itself; keep what you've loaded in your own state.

## Keyboard

The keys work as in the [week and day](/docs/views/week-and-day#keyboard), the
[month](/docs/views/month#keyboard) and the [agenda](/docs/views/agenda#keyboard), over the dates
on screen. In the week and day views, moving past the last visible day, or pressing Page Down,
scrolls a whole page; in the month, to the next month. In the agenda, keys that page past the range
move the calendar on. Days and weeks rendered around the visible ones are hidden from screen
readers, so the grid reads as one week, one day, or six weeks. Focus on an all-day bar stays on it
as the day it was focused on scrolls away.

## Dragging

Drag, resize, and create work as in the paged views. Dragging toward an edge keeps scrolling into
the days or weeks beyond, rendering them as it goes: the week and day views sideways, and the hours
up and down. The views stop snapping while a gesture is under way and come to rest on the nearest
page when you let go. The `dragPreview` option picks [snapped or free
drags](/docs/interactions#snapped-and-free-drags) here too.

## How it's built

The views put their scrolling in plain TypeScript that needs an element and no framework, and the
`.tsx` files bind it to Solid:

| File                            | Holds                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `scrolling-axis-controller.ts`  | The week, day and month's axis: days or weeks on a canvas thousands of items long, and where a scroll comes to rest |
| `scrolling-axis.tsx`            | `useScrollingAxis`, the axis in a Solid component, and the snap points                                              |
| `scrolling-time-grid.ts`        | The week and day's days on the axis, where each page starts, and where focus on a bar goes                          |
| `time-grid-scroll.ts`           | The sticky header, the header and all-day row following the days, and where the hours open                          |
| `scrolling-time-grid-scroll.ts` | Those in a Solid component                                                                                          |
| `scrolling-month.ts`            | The month's weeks on the axis, where each month starts, and how many event lanes fit                                |
| `agenda-scroll.ts`              | The agenda's scroll position and anchor date, which follow each other                                               |
| `use-agenda-scroll.ts`          | The agenda's scrolling in a Solid component, on the agenda's virtualizer                                            |

The views render only the days or weeks near their load range. The header and the all-day row follow
the days by a transform that a scroll timeline drives, where the browser has one, so they never lag
behind a swipe. The gutter's [secondary time zones](/docs/views/week-and-day#secondary-time-zones)
follow the first visible day on which neither zone changes its clocks. Scroll positions are never
signals: the views render again only when the anchor moves. In right-to-left layouts, pass
`direction="rtl"` to `Root`, which the week and day views need to scroll their days from the right.
The month and agenda follow `dir="rtl"` on the page or on `Calendar.Frame` too.
