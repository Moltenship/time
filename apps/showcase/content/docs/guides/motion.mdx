<MotionPreview />

Click a meeting: its chip grows into a card, and its title flies into the card's heading. Close
the card and it shrinks back into the meeting. Drag a meeting: a copy of it trails the pointer on a
spring, tilting with its speed, shows the time it would land at, and settles into the slot when
you let go, or flies back after Escape.

The calendar doesn't animate any of this itself. [Motion](https://motion.dev) does, through hooks
the calendar keeps for it:

- Every event is a `[data-event-id]` button, and its title is marked
  `data-slot="calendar-event-title"` in every view, so a transition can find both.
- `monitorCalendarDrags` tells a follower where the pointer is, what the preview shows, and where
  the drop landed.

Nothing in the calendar's source changes, and the calendar's packages don't depend on Motion.

## A meeting grows into its card

Motion's [`animateView`](https://motion.dev/docs/animate-view) runs a state update inside a view
transition, and `.add(from, to)` morphs one element into another. `grow` morphs the clicked chip
into the card and the chip's title into the card's heading, and `shrink` morphs them back:

<include meta='title="motion-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/motion-calendar.tsx#morph</include>

- The update runs in `flushSync`, so React has rendered the card when the browser captures the new
  state, and then waits a task, because Base UI places a popover just after it mounts.
- The box grows on a spring while what's inside it swaps with a short fade: early when it opens,
  late when it closes, so neither the chip's text nor the card's is ever stretched.
- In a browser without view transitions, `animateView` only runs the update, and the card opens
  at once.

A view transition draws each side of a morph as a picture stretched to the moving box. That suits
the card, whose insides swap while it's small, but would blow the title's text up as its box
widens. The title's morph gets the class `morph-text`, and one rule in your CSS keeps each side of
it at its own size:

```css title="app.css"
::view-transition-old(.morph-text),
::view-transition-new(.morph-text) {
  width: auto;
  height: auto;
}
```

The card is an ordinary Base UI popover. Its `data-slot`s are what `grow` looks for, and closing it
calls `shrink`:

<include meta='title="motion-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/motion-calendar.tsx#details</include>

Open the card from a click on the viewport. Every view renders events as `[data-event-id]`
buttons, so one listener opens any of them, and `eventIdOf` reads the meeting's ID:

<include meta='title="motion-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/motion-calendar.tsx#root</include>

While a view transition runs, about a third of a second, the page takes no clicks. That suits
opening a card. It doesn't suit the calendar's own navigation, which you click through quickly, so
leave paging and view switches without transitions.

## A card follows each drag

`monitorCalendarDrags` hears each meeting a view starts to move, and the follower you return
hears the rest of the drag:

| Follower method      | Called                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `move(point)`        | On every pointer move, with the pointer in viewport coordinates                                                                                                                   |
| `preview(preview)`   | Whenever the view's preview changes: its boxes (`rects`), the time it shows (`label`), and whether a policy would reject or flag it (`validity`). `null` when the view shows none |
| `end(dropped, drop)` | When the drag ends. `drop` is the preview the release proposed the move at, or `null` when it proposed nothing, as after Escape                                                   |

The follower here has no `claims`, so it only watches: the drag works as it would without it. The
card is a Motion element whose position follows the pointer through a spring, and whose rotation
follows the spring's speed. When the drag ends, it flies to the drop's first box, or back to the
meeting when nothing was dropped or a policy would reject the drop:

<include meta='title="motion-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/motion-calendar.tsx#drag</include>

The calendar's own preview still marks the slot under the card. The viewport above makes it faint
and drops its shadow, through the preview's custom properties.

## Without JavaScript

The preview itself takes CSS. The pointer layer moves it with `transform`, so the independent
`scale`, `rotate` and `translate` properties add to that rather than replace it. This lifts and
tilts it as it's picked up, with no Motion at all:

```css title="app.css"
[data-calendar-ghost="event"] {
  rotate: -2deg;
  scale: 1.05;
  transition:
    rotate 150ms ease-out,
    scale 150ms ease-out;

  @starting-style {
    rotate: 0deg;
    scale: 1;
  }
}
```

`--calendar-ghost-opacity`, `--calendar-ghost-shadow`, `--calendar-ghost-background` and
`--calendar-ghost-radius` restyle it, and `data-invalid` and `data-warning` mark a drop the policies
would reject or flag. The [theme](/docs/theming)'s reduced-motion rules already stop transitions on
the preview.

## Reduced motion and accessibility

- `morph` checks `prefers-reduced-motion` and only runs the update when it's set. `DragCard`
  follows no drag then, and the calendar's preview does the job alone.
- Focus never waits on an animation. Base UI moves focus into the card as it opens and back to the
  meeting as it closes, and the calendar's `Announcer` speaks each drag as it happens.
- The drag card is `aria-hidden`: it repeats what the preview and the announcements already say.
