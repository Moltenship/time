<DndKitPreview />

Drag a task from the backlog onto the week: the week shows the slots it would take, from the slot
under the pointer, and it becomes a meeting there when you let go. Drag a meeting onto the backlog
and it becomes a task again. The all-day row turns red under a task, because a policy only takes
timed meetings.

Your app already uses [dnd-kit](https://dndkit.com) for its own lists, so the backlog's cards are
ordinary dnd-kit draggables. `@moltenship/calendar-dnd-kit` connects them to the calendar: a drop
goes through the calendar's slots, policies and `onEventChange` like any drag. The calendar's own
drags, moving and resizing meetings inside the week, stay on its own pointer layer. The
[API reference](/docs/api/dnd-kit) lists every export and option.

## The calendar

week's all-day row takes no task, and its preview shows that:

## A task becomes a meeting

Each card spreads `calendarDropData` into its `data`: the record the task becomes, how long it
takes, and a title for screen readers. The record's times are placeholders; the drop decides when
the meeting is.

<include meta='title="backlog-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/dnd-kit-backlog.tsx#task</include>

Put one `DragDropProvider` around both the backlog and the calendar, and `CalendarDndKit` inside
it. `onEventChange` gets a dropped task as a create whose `event` is the record. A range dragged
out in the week is a create without one, which this example leaves alone:

<include meta='title="backlog-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/dnd-kit-backlog.tsx#root</include>

The create runs through the root's policies first, so the drop on the all-day row is rejected and
the task stays in the backlog. The calendar's `Announcer` names the task as it moves over the week
and says where it was added.

## A meeting goes back to the backlog

While a meeting is dragged out of the week, it's a dnd-kit drag of type `calendarEventType`, so the
backlog takes it with `accept`, and lights up with `isDropTarget`. Dropped there, the drag ends on
the backlog and the calendar proposes nothing. The backlog proposes the delete itself, so the
policies run and the meeting hides while it saves, and turns it into a task once it's saved:

<include meta='title="backlog-calendar.tsx"'>../../examples/dnd-kit-backlog.tsx#backlog</include>

Any other end of that drag, a drop in the week or Escape, ends it as `canceled`.

## Keyboard and touch

- dnd-kit's keyboard sensor drags tasks in: focus a card, press Space, move it with the arrow
  keys, Shift for bigger steps, and press Space again to drop it. The week previews and announces
  each step. Meetings can't be dragged out by keyboard, so give tasks and meetings a button of their
  own too, as the [demo](https://moltenship-time-demo.vercel.app) does: "Schedule" on a task opens
  the event editor, and "Unschedule" in a meeting's popover moves it back.
- On a touch screen, cards use dnd-kit's touch activation and meetings the calendar's long-press.
- dnd-kit's own announcements name draggables by ID. The example gives its `Accessibility` plugin
  announcements that name tasks and meetings, and stay quiet where the calendar's `Announcer`
  speaks for itself:
